# N°1 Academy — module overview video scripts

One overview video per module, for an AI avatar tool (HeyGen, Synthesia or Higgsfield). Each script runs about 3 minutes at a normal speaking pace. The video plays at the top of the module's first lesson, so it introduces the module rather than repeating every lesson.

## How to produce them

1. Paste the **Say** lines into the avatar tool as the script. Lines in *[Screen: …]* are on-screen text or B-roll for the editor. Don't read them aloud.
2. Use the same avatar, voice and background for all 8 videos so the course feels like one series.
3. Keep the AI disclosure card in the first 5 seconds of every video ("Presented by an AI avatar. Written and reviewed by the N°1 team."). The course teaches students to be honest with their audience about AI, so the course itself has to do the same.
4. For Spanish and Portuguese, use the tool's translation feature, then have a fluent speaker check the result before you publish. Module and lesson titles are already translated in `content/academy/lessons.es.ts` and `lessons.pt.ts`. Match those wordings.
5. Upload the video (YouTube unlisted works well), then paste the link into `content/academy/videos.ts` under the module number and language. The video appears on the site automatically.

## Module 01 — Foundations of AI Marketing

*[Screen: disclosure card, then title "Module 01: Foundations of AI Marketing"]*

**Say:**
Welcome to the N°1 Academy. Before we touch a single tool, we're going to get clear on what AI actually is, and what it isn't.

Most of the AI tools you'll use are built on large language models. They're trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next most likely word. That one idea explains almost everything. They're brilliant at writing text that *sounds* right. But they don't know facts the way a database does, and they don't check their own work unless you ask them to.

*[Screen: "Generative = creates. Predictive = scores and forecasts."]*

You'll hear two kinds of AI in this course. Generative AI creates something new: a caption, an email, an image. Predictive AI scores or forecasts: which lead is most likely to buy, or which ad will get the cheapest click. You've probably used predictive AI for years without calling it that.

*[Screen: funnel graphic — Awareness, Consideration, Conversion, Retention]*

Here's the most important habit in this module. Don't start with the tool. Start with the funnel. Ask: where are we losing the most time, or the most customers? For a lot of small businesses, the answer is speed. A lead fills out a form, and nobody replies for hours. That's where AI and automation pay off first.

*[Screen: "AI drafts. A human decides."]*

We'll also cover using AI responsibly: protecting customer data, never inventing reviews or testimonials, checking facts, and keeping a person in charge of anything your customers will see. That's the rule for the whole course. AI drafts. A human decides.

At the end of this module, you'll do an AI opportunity audit on a real business. You'll list ten tasks your team does every week, note how long each one takes, and pick the two where AI saves the most time with the least risk. Keep it. You'll build on it in every module after this one.

Let's get started.

## Module 02 — AI Tools & Automation

*[Screen: disclosure card, then "Module 02: AI Tools & Automation"]*

**Say:**
New AI tools launch every week. You don't need most of them. In this module, you'll learn to choose tools by the job they do, and then put them to work with automation.

*[Screen: "One assistant. One design tool. One automation platform."]*

A small business can do excellent AI marketing with three tools: one AI assistant for writing and thinking, one design tool, and one automation platform like Zapier, Make or n8n. Before you pay for anything new, test it on a real task from your business, not the demo. If you can't measure the difference, you don't need it.

*[Screen: "When ___, do ___, but only if ___."]*

Then we get to automation. This is where AI stops being a chat window and starts doing work while you sleep. Every workflow is built from the same parts. A trigger starts it. Actions do the work. Filters decide whether to continue. And AI steps summarize, sort or draft along the way. If you can say your workflow in one sentence, "When this happens, do that, but only if this is true," you can build it.

*[Screen: example workflow — form → CRM → AI summary → text to sales owner]*

We'll walk through a real example: a new lead fills out your form, it's saved to your CRM, AI summarizes what they want and rates them hot, warm or cold, and your sales owner gets a text in seconds.

Then you'll learn to build safely. Map the process by hand first. Start small. Make AI output predictable with a fixed format and a fallback. Keep a human approval step on anything customer-facing. And test with messy data before you go live.

Your exercise: design your first workflow on paper, or build it if you have the tools. Let's go.

## Module 03 — Prompt Engineering

*[Screen: disclosure card, then "Module 03: Prompt Engineering"]*

**Say:**
The difference between generic AI output and output that sounds like your business is almost always the prompt. This module teaches you to write prompts that work the first time, and keep working.

*[Screen: "Role · Context · Task · Format · Constraints · Examples"]*

A strong prompt has six parts. A role: who the AI should act as. Context: your business, your customer, your goal. The task: exactly what you want. The format: length, structure, tone. Constraints: what to avoid. And examples of what good looks like. Leave any of these out, and the AI fills the gap with something average.

*[Screen: before/after prompt comparison]*

Next, you'll learn the techniques that improve every output. Show examples instead of describing them. Ask the AI to ask you questions first. Break big jobs into smaller steps. Have it critique its own draft. And when a draft is weak, don't just say "make it better." Say exactly what's missing.

*[Screen: "Brand voice guide + prompt library = team standard"]*

Finally, we'll turn your best prompts into assets. A brand voice guide captures how your business sounds: the traits, the words you use and avoid, and the rules about claims. A shared prompt library turns one person's best prompts into the whole team's standard.

Your exercise: write your brand voice guide and your first three tested prompts. You'll use them for the rest of the course, and long after it.

## Module 04 — AI Content Strategy

*[Screen: disclosure card, then "Module 04: AI Content Strategy"]*

**Say:**
AI makes it easy to produce a lot of content. That's exactly the danger. More content isn't the goal. The right content, in your voice, is.

*[Screen: "Goal → Audience → Pillars"]*

So we start with strategy. What's the business goal, and who exactly are we trying to reach? From there, you'll choose three to five content pillars: recurring themes that connect what your audience needs with what you sell. You'll also use AI for real audience research, finding the common questions and exact phrases in your reviews and customer messages, with personal details removed.

*[Screen: repurposing pyramid — one pillar piece at the top, many pieces below]*

Then we scale. You'll learn the repurposing pyramid: one strong pillar piece, built on real expertise and real stories from the business, turned into emails, posts, short videos and more. AI does the heavy lifting on the repurposing. A human does the edit and the fact-check, every time. That edit is what protects your quality.

*[Screen: "Helpful, reliable, people-first"]*

We'll also cover search in the AI era. Google rewards helpful, reliable, people-first content, however it was made. What it punishes is hundreds of thin, near-identical pages. You'll learn what that means for AI content, and how to get your business mentioned in AI answers too.

Your exercise: one pillar, ten pieces. Let's build it.

## Module 05 — AI Analytics & Reporting

*[Screen: disclosure card, then "Module 05: AI Analytics & Reporting"]*

**Say:**
If you can't measure it, you can't improve it. And if you measure everything, you can't decide anything. This module is about measuring the few numbers that actually drive decisions.

*[Screen: "Vanity metrics vs. decision metrics"]*

Likes, followers and impressions feel good, but they rarely tell you what to do next. Decision metrics do: cost per lead, conversion rate, customer acquisition cost, and lifetime value. You'll learn each one in plain language, and you'll pick a single North Star: the one number that best shows whether the business is winning right now.

*[Screen: "Remove personal data. Check the math."]*

Then we put AI to work on your data. It's great at summarizing exports, spotting patterns and drafting reports. But it can also make confident mistakes. So you'll learn the habits that keep you safe: remove personal information before uploading anything, ask the AI to show its calculation, and spot-check any surprising number yourself. And remember, two things happening together doesn't mean one caused the other.

*[Screen: test-and-learn loop — hypothesis, test, measure, decide]*

Finally, you'll build a one-page weekly report that leads to decisions, not just numbers. And you'll learn the test-and-learn loop: change one thing at a time, wait for enough data, then decide.

Your exercise: your own measurement plan. After this module comes the capstone, so let's make it count.

## Module 07 — Google Business Profile & Local SEO

*[Screen: disclosure card, then "Hands-On Track · Module 07: Google Business Profile & Local SEO"]*

**Say:**
Welcome to the Hands-On Track. These are the skills small businesses pay for, starting with the one that brings in customers fastest: getting found on Google.

*[Screen: "Relevance · Distance · Prominence"]*

Google says local results are based on three things: relevance, distance and prominence. You can't move your building, but you can control a lot of the rest. That starts with your Google Business Profile.

*[Screen: example profile — correct name, primary category, hours, photos]*

You'll learn to set one up properly, step by step. Verify it. Get the basics exactly right: the real business name with no extra keywords stuffed in, and the right primary category, the single most important setting for relevance. Then fill in everything else: services, hours, photos and description. And keep it active with fresh photos and posts.

*[Screen: "Same name, address and phone everywhere"]*

Then we go beyond the profile. Your business details need to match everywhere they appear online, on Yelp, Facebook, directories and your own website. When they match, Google trusts them. When they don't, you lose ground. We'll also cover how your website supports your profile, and how to show up in AI answers too.

Your exercise: a local visibility audit and a fix list for a real business. This one pays off fast.

## Module 08 — Reviews & Reputation

*[Screen: disclosure card, then "Hands-On Track · Module 08: Reviews & Reputation"]*

**Say:**
Reviews are often the last thing a customer checks before they call you, or call your competitor. In this module, you'll build a system that brings in more reviews and handles every one of them well.

*[Screen: "Ask everyone. The same way. No incentives."]*

First, the rules, and they're not optional. Ask every customer the same way. Don't offer discounts or gifts for reviews. Don't only ask the happy ones, which is called review gating. And never have staff or anyone else write fake reviews.

*[Screen: "Right after the customer gets what they came for"]*

Then you'll learn how to get more reviews the honest way. Get your short review link. Ask at the right moment, right after the customer got the result they came for. And make it easy, with a text, a QR code or a card. Automation can send the request for you, as long as it asks everyone.

*[Screen: "AI drafts the reply. You decide."]*

Finally, responding. Thank people for good reviews in a way that doesn't sound copied and pasted. For bad reviews, start by thanking them for taking the time to write. Stay calm, and take the details offline. AI is great at drafting these replies, but a person checks and edits every one before it's posted. You'll also use AI to find patterns across your reviews, so you can fix the problems customers keep mentioning.

Your exercise: build a complete review system for a real business. Let's finish strong.
